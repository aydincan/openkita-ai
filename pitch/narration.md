# OpenKita AI — narration script (English)

Target length ~3:00. Spoken text: **462 words**. Rendered audio: **184.7 s** (incl. short pauses).

The narration is the source for the voice-over (`audio/`), the subtitles (`openkita-ai-pitch.srt`) and the speaker notes in the deck.
Prices and the hardware bundle are hypotheses to validate; the idea is not yet validated.

## Slide 1 — Title / promise  _(11.2 s)_

This is OpenKita AI: an open-source, self-hosted AI assistant for German Kitas and childcare providers. Less paperwork. Better communication. More time for children.

## Slide 2 — The problem  _(8.7 s)_

Kita teams spend hours on paperwork: letters, minutes, checklists, and the same questions again and again. Every hour at the desk is an hour away from the children.

## Slide 3 — What OpenKita AI is (and is not)  _(11.3 s)_

OpenKita AI is not another Kita management system. It is an assistant that eases existing administration, designed to integrate with open-source Kita software such as KitaLux.

## Slide 4 — Three capabilities  _(17.7 s)_

Three capabilities are planned. A Parent Communication Assistant drafts newsletters and invitations in German and English, with experimental Turkish and Arabic that need native review. An Administration Assistant turns notes into minutes and checklists. A Knowledge Assistant answers from your documents, with sources.

## Slide 5 — Mock-up: the summer party  _(11.3 s)_

Here is a mock-up of the planned flow, with fictional data. The manager asks for a friendly summer party announcement, with a request for buffet contributions. The assistant drafts a warm German text, ready to edit.

## Slide 6 — Translations and human approval  _(14.8 s)_

Then English, Turkish and Arabic versions appear. Turkish and Arabic are experimental: the default local model does not officially support them, so native review is required. Every draft is marked as AI-generated, and nothing is sent until a person approves it.

## Slide 7 — Privacy-first architecture  _(25.9 s)_

Version 0.1 is designed to be local-only: external AI is disabled and fails closed, there are no child profiles, and a person approves anything before it is sent. Planned for version 0.3: audit logs, and a Privacy Gateway that detects, minimises and verifies data before anything reaches an optional external provider, only after privacy tests. This reduces risk; it does not guarantee GDPR compliance.

## Slide 8 — Fully open source and tech stack  _(15.2 s)_

Everything is open source, licensed under Apache 2.0: code, prompts, workflows, schema, API and Docker Compose deployment. No proprietary edition. Built with Next.js, FastAPI, PostgreSQL and Ollama. Model weights have their own licences.

## Slide 9 — Business model (services)  _(11.9 s)_

The software is free. Revenue comes from services: installation, configuration, integration, training, support and migration. A test installation for one to three thousand euros is a hypothesis to validate.

## Slide 10 — OpenKita Box (hardware idea)  _(12.6 s)_

One idea is the OpenKita Box, a private AI appliance installed on premises. Prices are illustrative hypotheses, hardware obligations still need investigation, and we would sell to providers first.

## Slide 11 — Validation plan and honesty  _(14.8 s)_

Now the honest part: this is not yet validated. Version 0.1 is in progress: the foundation is built, and the AI features are in development. First we will interview five Kita managers, two providers and two educators, and ask what builds trust.

## Slide 12 — Roadmap  _(13.9 s)_

The roadmap: version 0.1 is in progress, then the Knowledge Assistant, production readiness with the planned Privacy Gateway, integrations, and a multi-facility 1.0 release. Later: Austria, Switzerland and wider Europe.

## Slide 13 — Call to action  _(15.5 s)_

Our ask: Kita managers and Träger, join a thirty-minute interview to help us validate OpenKita AI. The code is on GitHub; the link and a QR code are on screen. Less paperwork. Better communication. More time for children.
